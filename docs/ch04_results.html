Segmentation results, from summary data¶

This notebook compares the latest full run (v17, 15 October 2026) with the first (v16, September 2026), recomputes Table 4.6 of Chapter 4 (which reports v16), and draws the sample comparisons that led from one to the other. All figures come from the summary tables in data/.

Read this first.

  • The CSVs in data/ were transcribed by hand from the recorded screen output of the runs, not exported by the scripts. Their provenance is in data/README.md. The checks below confirm that the figures in the chapter follow from these tables; they do not re-run the pipeline.
  • Every length figure compares a clip with the length its identifier states. That tests consistency with scheduled durations, not the position of a cut point.
  • "Sample" is every eleventh recording of the delivery (151 recordings), used to test each change before the v17 full run.

Run settings: docs/method.md.

import pandas as pd

def row(run, group):
    r = own[(own.run == run) & (own.group == group)]
    assert len(r) == 1, (run, group)
    return r.iloc[0]

3. The 2025–2026 gap was a timing mismatch (sample)¶

In the September run, clips from 2025 and 2026 fell well below earlier years. The cause was in the files: their video stream starts later than the audio, and the picture detector had been looking at the wrong frames. Below, the same 151 recordings before the October changes (s_prec) and with both of them (s_v3), clips of every place in the break.

4. Why the strict acceptance rule stays¶

Each break was fitted again five and seven minutes away from where it belongs, inside the programme. A full match there is an accident. Looser rules keep slightly more breaks where they belong and let in many more accidents.
